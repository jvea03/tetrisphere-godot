Egg Escape: Block Puzzle Match – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 November 2026

Egg Escape: Block Puzzle Match ("the game") is published by Little Guy Games ("we", "us"). This policy explains what information the game handles and why. The short version: the game keeps your progress on your own device and does not ask you to create an account. What leaves your device is your leaderboard name, critter and star totals, stickers you send, anonymous gameplay statistics (which you can turn off), and what Google's advertising and analytics services, Meta's ad measurement (on Android) and the app stores' purchase services need to work.

Information stored on your device

Your progress – level reached, coins, boosters, critters, ship parts, chests, streaks, settings and similar game state – is saved in a file on your device. It is not uploaded to us. Uninstalling the game deletes it. The game does not ask for, or store, your name, email address, contacts, location, photos or any other personal information.

Clubs shown in the game are generated on your device; nothing about them is sent to a server.

Leaderboards and stickers (Google Firebase)

The leaderboards are online, run on Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Firebase Authentication). When you play, the game signs your device in anonymously – no email, phone number or Google account – which gives it a random player ID. It then stores, for each daily, weekly and season leaderboard you play on: the player name you chose in the game, the critter you picked as your picture, and your star total. Other players see these on the leaderboards.

From a leaderboard you can send another player one of the game's stickers. A sticker carries your player name and critter to that player, and is deleted from the server once their game has shown it. There is no free text: stickers are fixed pictures. You can block a player, which hides them and their stickers on your device, and report a player's name, which sends us their player ID and name to review.

Leaderboard rows are kept while their board is shown and for record keeping afterwards; you can ask us to delete yours at the address below (tell us your player name). Firebase processes this data under the Google Privacy Policy and Firebase's data processing terms.

The game also downloads a small file of announcements and gifts from GitHub (a request like any web page, which shows GitHub your IP address). It sends no player data, and your device decides which messages to show.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The game shows rewarded and interstitial ads served by Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address, coarse location derived from it, device and app information, and how you interact with ads. This data is processed by Google under the Google Privacy Policy and AdMob's data practices.

On iPhone and iPad the game first asks, through Apple's prompt, whether it may track you across other companies' apps and websites for advertising; if you say no, ads are not personalised with your device's advertising identifier. Where required (for example in the EEA and UK), the game also shows a consent form before any ad is loaded and respects your choice. You can change that choice later in Settings, where it applies. If you buy the No Ads Pass, the game stops requesting ads.

Purchases (Apple App Store and Google Play)

In-app purchases are processed by the Apple App Store on iPhone and iPad and by Google Play on Android. We never see or store your payment details. The store provides the game with a purchase record (product and order identifier) so that the items can be granted and restored on this device. Your payment data is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy or the Google Privacy Policy.

Analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

On Android, iPhone and iPad, the game uses Google Analytics for Firebase to understand how players use it: for example, when the game is first opened, which levels are won or lost, and in-app purchases. Firebase may collect an app instance ID (and on Android, your device's advertising ID; on iPhone and iPad it does not use the advertising identifier), your IP address and a coarse location derived from it, device and app information, and these in-game events. We use this to improve the game and to measure which of our ads bring players who actually play. The data is processed by Google under the Google Privacy Policy and Firebase's data processing terms, and it does not include your name, email address or contacts.

Ad measurement (Meta)

On Android, the game includes the Meta (Facebook) SDK so that we can tell whether people who saw our ads on Facebook or Instagram went on to install and open the game. While it is running, the SDK sends Meta that the game was installed or opened, the game version, your device's advertising ID, your IP address and a coarse location derived from it, device and operating system details, and, for in-app purchases made on Android, the product and price. You do not need a Facebook or Instagram account, and the game never sends Meta your leaderboard name, anything you type, or your contacts. Meta may use this data to measure and improve our ads and, under its own policies, for its own purposes, including showing you ads. It processes the data under Meta's Privacy Policy.

In the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area and Switzerland the SDK does not start, and nothing is sent to Meta, unless you tap Allow on the consent card. Everywhere else it runs by default. Wherever you are, you can turn it off at any time in the game's Settings → Share usage data, and you can reset or limit your advertising ID in your device's Settings. The iPhone and iPad version does not include the Meta SDK.

Gameplay analytics (GameAnalytics)

On Android, iPhone and iPad, the game sends anonymous gameplay statistics to GameAnalytics (GameAnalytics ApS, Denmark) so we can see where the game is too hard, too easy or broken: sessions and how long they last, levels started, won and lost (with the time taken and hearts left), first-launch steps, ads shown, in-app purchases (product and price), and the game's own error messages. Each event carries a random ID created on your device, the game version, your device model and operating system version, and whether you have bought anything. It never includes your advertising ID, your leaderboard name, anything you type or your contacts. GameAnalytics processes this data on our behalf under its own privacy policy.

In the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, the game asks first and sends nothing (to GameAnalytics or to Google Analytics for Firebase) unless you tap Allow. Everywhere else it is on by default. Wherever you are, you can turn it off or on at any time in the game's Settings → Share usage data; turning it off also deletes the random ID from your device.

Google Play and App Store Connect may also share aggregated install, usage and crash statistics with us; these do not identify you.

Children

The game is intended for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your choices

  • Reset or limit ad personalisation: device Settings → Google → Ads on Android, Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking on iPhone and iPad. For Meta's ad data, use Meta's ad preferences.
  • Stop gameplay analytics and Meta ad measurement: the game's Settings → Share usage data.
  • Delete all game data on your device: uninstall the game, or clear its data in device Settings → Apps.
  • Delete your leaderboard rows: email us at the address below with your player name.
  • Stay off the leaderboards' names: keep the default name, or choose one that doesn't identify you.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts handling data differently, we will update this page and the date at the top before the change ships.
